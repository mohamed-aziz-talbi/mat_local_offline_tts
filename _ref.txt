LOCAL AI FOR STARTUPS
A Business Intelligence study of economic, technical and environmental sustainability
PROJECT OVERVIEW
This project evaluates whether locally hosted or hybrid artificial intelligence can help startups and small businesses control
operating costs, reduce dependence on commercial APIs, retain operational control and minimise environmental impact.
UPDATED RESEARCH QUESTION
To what extent can local and hybrid artificial intelligence solutions help startups reduce operating costs and
dependence on commercial APIs while maintaining adequate performance and minimising environmental
impact?
Evaluation dimensions: economic sustainability  technical performance  environmental sustainability  operational autonomy

1. Topic and Context
AI providers such as OpenAI, Anthropic and Google offer models through paid subscriptions and APIs. These services support
writing, document analysis, customer assistance, data processing and report generation. Costs may grow with usage, and
APIs may impose rate limits or quotas.
An alternative is to run models on hardware owned or controlled by the organisation. Local deployment can reduce some
variable costs and increase control over data, but it also introduces hardware investment, electricity consumption, maintenance
and infrastructure-management requirements. Cloud, local and hybrid strategies should therefore be compared rather than
assuming one is always best.
2. Motivation
 Economic: assess affordability for startups with limited budgets.
 
 Technical: evaluate model capability, latency, throughput, quality and reliability.
 
 Environmental: estimate energy use and carbon emissions, including relevant hardware lifecycle considerations.
 
 Decision-making: provide evidence to choose a cloud, local or hybrid architecture.
 
3. Research Problem
To what extent can local and hybrid AI help startups and small businesses reduce operating costs, limit
dependence on commercial APIs, maintain performance suited to their needs and minimise environmental
impact?
The research covers four dimensions:
 Economic: total cost of ownership, cost per task, savings and payback period.
 
 Technical: latency, throughput, answer quality and reliability.
 
 Environmental: energy consumption, estimated greenhouse gas emissions and hardware lifecycle.
 
 Operational autonomy: API dependence, rate limits, data control and service availability.

Experimental consistency: compare equivalent tasks using the same test dataset and consistent evaluation criteria. If
company data is unavailable, build a documented experimental dataset and state assumptions explicitly.
5. Data Transformation (ETL)
 Extract: gather provider prices, model characteristics, test results, energy measurements, hardware data and electricity
 carbon-intensity factors.
 Transform: clean records, standardise units, handle missing values, align time periods and calculate cost and environmental
 indicators.
 Load: store consolidated data in a database or warehouse for BI analysis.
 
Suggested tools: Python and Pandas for processing, SQL for storage, and Power BI for analysis and reporting.
Environmental data quality
Measure energy directly where possible using a power meter or reliable telemetry. If energy or cloud emissions must be
estimated, document the method, assumptions and uncertainty. Do not present estimates as direct measurements.
6. Data Warehouse Design
A star schema is a practical starting point. Dimension tables describe models, time and workload scenarios; fact tables store
performance, cost and environmental measurements.

 Star schema: straightforward and well suited to Power BI.
 
 Snowflake schema: normalises dimensions into additional related tables.
 
 Fact constellation: uses multiple fact tables that share dimensions; useful if cost, performance and environmental data have
 different levels of detail.

Carbon calculation: estimated operational emissions can be calculated as energy consumed (kWh) × an appropriate
electricity emissions factor (kg CO■e/kWh). Choose a factor that matches the relevant location, period and accounting method.
Include uncertainty and avoid treating this simplified calculation as a complete lifecycle assessment.
Fair comparison: include hardware purchase and depreciation, electricity, maintenance and administration time for local
deployment. For cloud deployment, include subscriptions and API charges. Hardware manufacturing, data-centre overheads
and cooling should be included when credible data is available; otherwise document the boundary and limitations.
8. Power BI Dashboards
8.1 Executive / Decision Dashboard
 Monthly cost, estimated savings, break-even point, return on investment and cost by workload volume.
 
8.2 Technical Performance Dashboard
 Model comparison by answer quality, latency, throughput, error rate and reliability.
 
8.3 Environmental Impact Dashboard
 Energy use, estimated CO■e per 1,000 successful tasks, energy efficiency by model, and sensitivity to different electricity
 carbon-intensity factors.
8.4 Operational Autonomy Dashboard
 Request volumes, resource utilisation, errors, API-limit events and external-service dependence, where data is available.
 
A useful cross-dashboard visual is a cost-versus-carbon comparison, helping decision-makers examine financial and
environmental trade-offs together.

9. Data Mining and Decision Support
 Regression: estimate monthly cost or energy use as a function of request volume and other relevant variables.
 
 K-means clustering: group workload scenarios by cost, performance, energy use and operational requirements.
 
 Break-even analysis: estimate the workload volume at which local deployment becomes economically attractive under
 stated assumptions.
 Workload forecasting: estimate future resource needs if sufficient historical data is available.
 
 Multi-criteria decision analysis: compare cloud, local and hybrid options using transparent criteria for cost, performance,
 autonomy and environmental impact.
The final objective is a decision-support tool that recommends an architecture based on a company’s budget, workload, quality
requirements, data constraints and sustainability goals.
Methodological Recommendations
For scientifically meaningful results, run repeatable tests on several local models and at least one cloud-based service, using
comparable tasks and a documented evaluation protocol. Compare solutions at a similar level of answer quality and report the
hardware, software versions, workload, duration and measurement method.
Local deployment is not automatically cheaper or greener. It uses electricity and hardware that must be manufactured,
maintained and eventually replaced. Cloud infrastructure also consumes resources, but provider-specific energy and emissions
data may be unavailable. The study should report evidence, assumptions and uncertainty instead of claiming a universal
winner.
Suggested Project Scope
 BI-only study: analyse public and documented data, clearly identifying assumptions and gaps.
 
 BI + experimental prototype: run a small local model and a cloud alternative, measure comparable tasks and use the results
 in the dashboards. This can provide stronger empirical evidence if time, hardware and service access allow.
